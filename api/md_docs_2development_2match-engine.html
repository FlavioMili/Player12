Match engine: implementation and rationale

The match engine is the part of the game engine that turns lineups, tactics, player attributes and a seed into a match. The wider career engine advances days and applies its results; see Architecture. For individual decisions, read Player behavior.

Ownership and data flow

flowchart TD
Career[Career players, lineups, tactics and fixture seed] --> Input[MatchSimulationInput]
Input --> Batch[MatchScheduler / MatchSimulation::run]
Batch --> Engine[MatchEngine]
Career --> Live[MatchScene configures a live engine]
Live --> Engine
Engine --> Snapshot[MatchRenderSnapshot]
Snapshot --> Views[2D / 3D views]
Engine --> Result[MatchReport and player consequences]
Result --> Apply[Career applies the result]

MatchEngine owns match slots, the ball, copied strategies, random-generator state, events and statistics. It borrows const Player* values from the lineups and a const StatsConfig&. Keep those objects alive and unchanged until the engine and any look-ahead copies are destroyed. A copied engine has independent simulation state but shares those read-only inputs. It is not a deep copy of a career.

MatchPlayer is a pitch slot, not the persistent Player. Substitution reuses the slot and gives the incoming player a new PlayerMatchStats entry; the outgoing player's statistics remain available. With walk-outs (Binturong out of play and both sides on the Binturong body) a substitution is set under way instead (PendingSubstitution): the player walks off at the nearest point of the boundary, the sub waits on the touchline (several queue), and the slot passes to the sub's own body (applySubstitution) once the player is wholly over the line; the restart waits until the sub is on, and after 60 s the two are put where they were going. Never identify a player's career by a vector index. Use PlayerID, and use statsIndex for the current occupant's match statistics.

The engine does not update the database or persistent player condition during play. MatchdaySquad::carryCondition() supplies starting condition; MatchdaySquad::consequences() extracts the outcome. Reports and consequences are applied by the career after simulation. This separation lets background matches read players concurrently without racing with career mutations.

Coordinates and clocks

Quantity Convention
Player/ball x, y Metres (PitchPoint): x from the left goal line along the engine's pitch length, y from the top touchline across its width (MatchPitch; a match is 105 × 68)
Attack direction Home toward x = length; away toward x = 0 throughout the match
Lineup coordinates Fractions of the pitch, own goal at x = 0 for both teams; placed through MatchPitch::at, away x mirrored when loaded
Velocity / acceleration Metres per simulated second / metres per second squared
Ball z Metres above the ground
RoleProfile distances Metres
Facing angle Radians on the ground: points along (cos, sin) in metres, so turn rates are the same whichever way a player faces
stepCounter Integer ticks of Timing::FIXED_STEP_SECONDS (0.1 s)
Match clock Minutes displayed for the current period, plus announced added time

Points and the steps between them are metres: point - point is a metric Vector2F, and point + vector is a point. distance and metricDirection measure between points. Geometry worked in Vector2F reads points through asVector(point) and writes them back with asPoint(vector) (both only change the type).

Tuning distances come in two kinds (training ground plan §17):

  • Metres (_METRES names): margins, buffers and distances from the ball, a player, a line or a goal, the same on any pitch. The markings come from the Laws: the goal (Shooting::GOAL_HALF_WIDTH_METRES), the penalty mark and area (Pitch::PENALTY_SPOT_METRES, PENALTY_AREA_DEPTH_METRES), the goal area. "Near a goal" tests measure from the goal line with pitch.fromOwnGoalLine(point, home) and pitch.toGoalLine(point, home).
  • Shares of the pitch, on purpose: the team's shape and its moves from the formation (the final third, attack channels, the defensive block, support and run advances, pass progression). These scale with the pitch. They become metres through pitch.at, atX and atY, and go back through fractionX/fractionY. The formation frame (tactics, handshake line-up, substitutions) keeps its own _SHARE copies of the player limits and the keeper's line-up spot.

Each engine holds its own pitch size (MatchPitch, MatchEngine::pitch): length, width and the values built from them (body limits inside the boards, the furniture round the pitch). Inside a MatchEngine member the pitch helpers (clampToPitch, the goal frames) are members that use it; a free function takes the pitch as its first argument (match_engine_internal.h). Engine code never reads MatchTuning::Pitch::MATCH_LENGTH_METRES / MATCH_WIDTH_METRES: those are the match pitch's size, the default, for code outside the engine (views, tactics screens, tools). The tuning's shares of the pitch are scaled by the engine's pitch; its metres are not.

There are three different notions of time:

  • Wall time is supplied by the frame loop. Playback speed controls how much simulated time the viewer requests.
  • Simulated time advances bodies, ball flight, cooldowns and input. Full fidelity uses a 0.1 s step; the free ball uses four 0.025 s substeps, and a controlled player's body uses five 0.02 s substeps. Tactical targets normally refresh every two ticks and also on possession/flight changes.
  • Match-clock time usually follows simulated time, excluding breaks. In Play, setPlayHalfMinutes() scales the clock by 45 / minutes while physical motion stays at the same speed. Load uses physical time; appearances and ball-in-play minutes follow the match clock. The engine accepts 3–15 minutes per half, with 0 for the watch clock.

This split makes short played halves possible without making players or shots move nine times faster. It is documented in the recovered gameplay design notes.

One authoritative simulation step

simulateStep() calls simulateStepBody() and then describes pending events. The body executes this sequence:

  1. Apply due manager commands at the current tick boundary, capture the previous interpolation frame, advance stepCounter, then apply due controller input.
  2. Validate the controlled player, age action/touch/dive timers and shouts, recover temporary formation familiarity and refresh effective team sliders.
  3. Update team phases and dispatch on MatchState.
  4. In PLAYING, update player targets and movement, advance the clock and load, resolve dribbling/challenges/actions, track pressure, then integrate the free ball if play still continues. Account for possession, advantage and injuries.
  5. Refresh ratings when due and check period completion on the ordinary path.

Goal celebrations, shootouts and half-time return from their own branches. Other restarts run substitution checks, reposition players, advance the clock when appropriate and call completeRestart() when ready. A goal celebration keeps the clock running; half-time and the shootout do not. With walk-outs, updateWalk() owns the start of each half (out of the tunnel, the first half's line-up and handshake) and half-time's walk into the tunnel; the substitutions under way move on every step (updateSubstitutions()).

In Binturong, crowd returns and steward throws follow an arc to the recorded restart spot and stop there. This includes a replacement ball thrown from the front row when the original goes out of sight. The taker heads to the spot during the return and uses the normal pickup or placement logic when the ball arrives.

Round the pitch

MatchGround builds the tunnel, the dugouts, the technical areas and the gaps in the boards from MatchTuning::Pitch and a pitch size (MatchGround::Layout). The engine's walls (keepOffGround()) and the dead ball read their engine's own (pitch.ground); both views read the match pitch's (MatchGround::Match). Besides the players, the engine moves touchlineBodies, with touchlinePeople saying who each is: each Binturong-bodied side's subs, staff and manager, and the match officials (match_touchline_people.cpp). They are MatchPlayer bodies with stand-in stats, so walking, run-ins, stumbles and falls use the players' own code; they referee and manage nothing (the engine decides as before). The match officials collide with no one: each steps out of players' way as they come (moveOfficial), and the referee picks a spot near his usual one clear of players (officialSpot). match_walk_out.cpp holds the walk-outs, walk-offs, substitutions on foot and sendings-off on foot (the player leaves at the nearest point of the boundary, as a substituted player does, and walks round the pitch's edge into the tunnel; the restart waits only until he is off the pitch). A body is one size everywhere, MatchTuning::Body.

Training pitches

A MatchPitch also says what the pitch looks like (PitchSurface::MATCH or TRAINING) and what it ends at (PitchEdge::LINES or WALLS). MatchPitch::training(length, width, goal) is a walled training pitch of any size, with a goal of a given GoalKind at each end or none; an engine takes its pitch at construction (the match pitch by default), and a drill declares its own (Drill::pitch()).

  • Goals. MatchPitch::goal (PitchGoal) holds the goal's half width, crossbar height, post and net depth; PitchGoal::ofKind gives the sizes, from a full-size goal (7.32 x 2.44 m) through 9v9, 7v7, futsal, 5-a-side and small framed goals down to a pop-up (1.2 x 0.8 m), with the post and net scaled to the height. Everything that reads the goal (the frames, the goal-line test, the keepers' angles, the brains' aim, the xG angle) reads the pitch's, so a match pitch is unchanged and a training goal needs no code of its own. The xG fit itself is the match one: its angle term shrinks with the goal but its distance terms do not, and it assumes a keeper. A pitch without a goal gives no chance at all (xG 0), so nobody shoots.
  • Walls. On a walled pitch the ball bounces off the edge every sub-step at any height, keeping the boards' restitution (bounceOffWalls). The walls are open across a goal's mouth, and behind the goal line the net is a closed box. The ball is never out of play: the only thing either out-of-play version checks is a goal, so there are no throw-ins or corners. A goal has a short stop (3 s, as in Play mode). Bodies stay a radius off the walls, on the side they are on: the players inside, a coach outside.
  • Laws. MatchPitch::laws (PitchLaws) says which Laws are played: offside, fouls, penalties and cards, and how play restarts. A match has them all and the Laws' restarts; PitchLaws::none(), a training pitch's default, has none and restarts by a coach's serve. A drill switches on what it needs. With fouls off a challenge that rolls a foul ends as if it had not (whistles), counted as a foul not given; offside needs both the pitch's Law and the drill rule (offsideCalled).
  • The coach's serve. A serving pitch restarts with MatchState::SERVE instead of a kick-off or a free kick or penalty: after a goal's stop everyone holds where he was, after a whistle (2 s) likewise, and the ball waits at the feet of the coach (TouchlineRole::SERVER), a touchline body at MatchPitch::serveStation outside the wall. He then plays it along the ground from just inside the wall, weighted as a pass, to a player of the side the Laws name (ServeSide: the side that conceded or was fouled, alternate, home or away; ServeReceiver: the most open player or the one nearest him), as a fed ball meant for that player. beginServe, updateServe and deliverServe in match_out_of_play_dispatch.cpp are shared by both out-of-play versions.
  • Nothing else round it. A training pitch has no furniture, no frames when it has no goals, and no touchline people but its coach.
  • Kit. MatchEngine::setKit lays named cones, lines, areas and dummies (PitchKit::Layout, match_layout.h) on the pitch, any pitch; the views draw them. Cones, lines and areas are drawn only. placeScenario clears them.
  • Dummies. A mannequin, a junior mannequin or an agility pole (PitchKit::Dummy; sizes in dummyShape, from makers' listings) is solid: an upright cylinder with a domed top, every point within its radius of its axis from the ground to its shoulder. The ball bounces off one live (the shared stretch, dummyInPath and bounceOffDummy, booked as a non-shot woodwork touch; a shot that hits one is a shot no longer) and dead. A player is kept out of one and bounces off it (bounceOffDummies, then on the Binturong body the jolt judged as a collision's); a player whose straight line to his target runs into one heads for its nearest edge for the step (steerRoundDummies; not one on a drill's route), and the Binturong dribble steers round them. Both brains leave out a pass a dummy blocks (passBlockedByDummy: a ground pass into one, a lofted one too low where it passes). Shots don't look for them yet.
  • Views. The render snapshot carries the size, surface, edge and kit. The 2D view fits a training pitch, with a 5 m grass margin, inside its area; the 3D view centres it where the match pitch sits (worldOf), so the cameras frame it unchanged. Both draw plain grass, the walls (a line in 2D, a net fence in 3D, open at the goal mouths), the goals at their size, the kit and the coach (a bright training top), and none of the stadium or markings.

The Walled box drill plays a few a side on one, with any goal, the serve's side and receiver, fouls off or whistled, and four dummies or none, and reports the wall bounces, goals, shots, serves, fouls and dummy hits (the Slalom can use poles for its cones); fm_lab drill --pitch LxW [--goal KIND] plays any drill on one (KIND is none, full, 9v9, 7v7, futsal, 5-a-side, small or pop-up).

The Small-sided game drill plays the brain redesign's ladder (1 player, 1 v 1, 2 v 2, 3 v 3, N v N; presets of those names) on a walled pitch sized by area per player (100 m² by default, length 1.5 x width), the coach serving to the home side to start (MatchEngine::serveToStart) and after goals. It reports goals, shots, possession, the ball loose, changes of side and passes, and each rung's measures (time to the ball and first shot; spells and tackles; swarming, press and cover, spread; team-mates in range). The baseline for both families is in the training ground plan, section 22.

Training sessions

A training session (tools/match_sandbox_session.h) runs several zones at once on one 105 x 68 training ground: a squad and a list of exercises, each exercise a set of zones, each zone a drill with its settings, placed (its centre, turned 0 or 90 degrees) with its players and, if wanted, its own brain and body. Each zone is its own engine with its own ball (a DrillRun on the session's own players, its own seed), and the zones of an exercise step together. checkSessionPlan refuses zones that don't fit or overlap, wrong player counts and players in two zones.

  • Carrying players over. MatchEngine::CarriedPlayer is a player's running state (position, velocity, facing, stamina, sprint reserve, burst debt, load speed); carriedPlayer reads it and carryPlayerIn makes a player take it up after a drill starts (his brain replans). A session keeps each player's state when he leaves a zone, so condition carries through the session. takeOffForDrill takes a player off mid-run (the changing room).
  • Changing exercise. When every zone is over: the next exercise's zones are made ready; one engine over the whole ground (walled at its fence, no goals, the ball held with a coach by holdPlayForRegroup) takes everyone who was playing, where he was; each walks to his next place with a drill target (the serve's wait lets a player with one walk to it); and when all are there (within 1 m, or after 60 s) the zones take their players as they arrived. Nobody jumps place or condition at the merge or the split.
  • The view. fillSessionRenderSnapshot makes one snapshot of the ground: every zone's players, coach, kit and ball placed on it, the zones' walls and goals (MatchRenderSnapshot::zones) and the other zones' balls (extraBalls); the picked zone's ball is the main one, which the 3D cameras follow. During a change it draws the merged engine.
  • One zone saved. A zone can be saved as far as it has played (ZoneRecording, tools/match_sandbox_zone_recording.h: its drill, versions, players, their carried-in starts, commands and fingerprints) and replayed alone on its own pitch on the current engine, showing where the engine first plays it differently. The parallel session itself isn't saved.

In the sandbox, sessions are on the Training page with the others (the first picks make up the squad); the session scene shows the ground in 2D or 3D, each zone's report, the changing room, and a ZONE card (the zone the cameras follow, its brain and body, its debugger, Save this zone). Saved zones open from the page's ZONE RECORDINGS card. fm_lab session "NAME" runs a session headless.

Order is part of behavior. Moving actions before players changes tackle reach; moving commands across the tick increment changes replay timing; continuing ball integration after a goal can resolve a second contact against dead play.

Choosing an advance API

API Intended caller and tradeoff
update(simulatedSeconds) Live catch-up with an accumulator; clamps large deltas and drops excess whole steps to keep the window responsive
advance(simulatedSeconds) Headless bounded run; rounds the request up to whole ticks and does not drop steps
simulateToEnd() Finish a watched match at FULL fidelity
simulateToEnd(BACKGROUND) Unwatched fixtures; merge up to three ticks only during selected dead-ball waits; disable detailed tracking
advancePlayback(wallSeconds) Live full-match/highlight presentation, including skips between predicted highlights

BACKGROUND uses the same open-play engine and rules, but its seeded result need not equal FULL: different restart stepping can change later play. Compare distributions with the lab, not scores seed by seed across fidelities. A controlled player forces full fidelity. simulateToEnd also has a finite iteration guard; callers investigating an engine defect should inspect getState() rather than treating a returned call as proof of full time.

Ball flight, contacts and rules

passBall() and strikeShot() turn an action into launch position, horizontal and vertical speeds and curve. stepFlight() supplies the drag, gravity, bounce and rolling model also used by flight prediction. updateBall() handles each short segment in order: integrate, resolve a shot at the keeper's plane, check goal/out-of-bounds, then resolve loose-ball contacts. Swept geometry checks the travelled segment so fast balls are not tested only at their end position. Aerial deliveries also go through resolveAerialContest().

setPossession() centralizes ownership, flight cleanup, touch statistics and team transitions. Use it when adding a new reception mechanism. launchBall() and clearFlightState() maintain kick/flight bookkeeping. Possession storage is private; new transitions must use the selected possession helpers, preserving receipt, flight and history together.

Expected goals estimates chance quality and influences shot selection. Goals are resolved through the struck ball, blocks, saves and goal-line crossing; the engine does not simply award a goal from random < xG. Saves still include attribute-based probabilistic handling after reach checks. The xG formula is an in-code heuristic, not the fitted model proposed in the early specification.

Goalkeeper reaction time starts when the shot is struck. Before the first dive update, the reaction clock ages by actual ball-flight substeps. This covers open-play launches, headers launched partway through flight, and restart shots that do not fly until the following tick. When the delay ends partway through a later tick, the keeper moves for its remaining time. Reach checks determine whether contact is physically possible; the stretch penalty then models handling difficulty within that reach.

setupThrowIn, setupCorner, setupFreeKick and the other setup functions own taker selection, placement and waiting. completeRestart owns release. Keep the restart state until the ball is kicked: it determines offside exemptions and set-piece classification. Pass offside is recorded at release and enforced on involvement; it must not be recomputed from receipt positions. An accidental defensive deflection preserves the attacking restart's origin. An own goal from that flight counts in the scoring team's set-piece total, but does not become an attacking shot, headed goal or converted penalty.

MatchRules holds calculations that can be tested without an engine: added time, period limits, fatigue, ratings, aerial reach, sanctions and shootout completion. Random rolls are passed into rules that need them. The stateful engine applies their results, logs incidents and decides which restart or period comes next.

Calibrating finishing without changing keeper reach

Shot placement uses a distance-scaled Gaussian error, with extra scatter from pressure and imperfect finishing. Shooting::ERROR_BASE_METRES controls the baseline scatter; Player::LEVEL_FINISHING_GAIN reduces precision below the reference league level. This lets league quality affect conversion without artificially changing how often teams shoot. Keeper handling is a separate roll after the ball enters the keeper's physical reach.

Check equal teams, mismatched teams, weaker versus stronger leagues, and a full league table together when changing these values. Repeat the same comparisons on independent seeds: a better result for one fixed sample can hide a regression in another. Keep the existing calibration bands and document the sampled inputs.

Determinism, replay and observation

For the consolidated possession record, per-match Aardvark/Binturong selection, const reader queries, boundary audit and paired --trace-possession command, see Possession. Stages 1–3 preserve existing behaviour; control/claim policy changes remain Stage 4 work.

The match uses seeded std::mt19937 streams for play and incidents, with in-tree sampling helpers. Hash/epoch noise supplies stable variation for some tactical choices. World simulation separately uses WorldRng. A seed alone does not reproduce a match after changing attributes, tuning, fidelity or command order. Preserve all those inputs when comparing behavior, and do not claim cross-build or cross-platform bit identity from a same-build replay test.

Manager changes are MatchCommandRecords; direct control uses MatchInputRecords. Both are tick-stamped and replayed by the engine. Manager commands execute before tick advancement; controller records apply afterwards. Use the public setters so logging, validation and highlight invalidation happen together. New commands also need a payload, execution branch and replay test. The logs are in-memory APIs, not the versioned replay-file format proposed by Spec Kitty.

Highlight prediction simulates an engine copy. Read-only inspection and rendering must not consume random draws. The current MatchRenderSnapshot copies positions but borrows player/event/stat pointers; consume it while its source is alive and stable. It is not an owned snapshot suitable for unsynchronized cross-thread publishing. The 2D and 3D views interpolate the same state.
