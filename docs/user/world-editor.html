Worlds: play, build and share

A world is everything a career starts from: countries, competitions, clubs, players, kits and league economies. The game ships with a built-in world, and you can play in worlds other players made, build your own, or combine several into one.

Start a career in another world

Every new career asks which world to play in, right after you choose the save slot:

  • Default world: the built-in world, generated fresh from a random seed.
  • Edit a world first: opens the World editor; press Start career there when you are done.
  • Your worlds: worlds you imported or saved. Select one to see its author, version and size, then press Start career.

To use a world someone shared, download its .fmdb file, then press Import file… or simply drop the file onto the game window. The file is checked before anything happens: if something is wrong you see each problem with the club, player or competition it concerns, and nothing is imported. The game never downloads files itself.

A world only affects the career you are starting. Your other careers keep the world they were created in, even if you later edit or delete that world. Clubs and players a world leaves out are generated as in the default world, so a world of a few clubs or a list of legendary players is enough.

Build a world

Open Main menu → World editor. It opens the built-in world; use New to start from a small example world or an empty one, or Open for one of your worlds or any .fmdb file.

The left side lists the layers of the world:

PageWhat you edit
World detailsName, author, version, description and the season ages refer to
LanguagesA language with the first and last names of its speakers
ConfederationsGroups of regions: the continental cups, national finals and trips of their national teams (cups are written as data)
RegionsA name and its confederation; pre-season tours stay inside a region
CountriesName, the people (demonym), language, region, football and economic level
Cities and TravelCities with their population and travel times between them (stored for future features; no effect on matches yet)
CompetitionsDivisions, the division above for promotion and relegation, tie-break rule and the economy and match profile
ClubsName, three-letter code, competition, city, colours and full kits, stadium
PlayersNames, nickname, club, age, nationality, position, foot, attributes and contract

Pick a page, search or filter the list (for example the players of one club, or free agents), and select an entry to edit it with the same controls as the in-game editor. Add, Duplicate and Delete work on the selected page; to move a club to another division or a player to another club, change its competition or club in the form. Deleting asks first and tells you what changes: the players of a deleted club become free agents, the clubs of a deleted division wait without a competition until you place them. Undo and Redo step through your changes.

Leagues you create follow their country's football and economic level: lower divisions are weaker and poorer. Set the profile fields of a competition only if you want to tune its finances or match style yourself.

Problems

The counters at the top show errors, problems that block a career and warnings; select one to see the list and jump to the entry concerned. You can save an unfinished world at any time. A career needs at least one division of four or more clubs, an even number of clubs per division, unique club codes and every club placed in a competition.

Combine worlds

Combine loads another world into the one you are editing, like building blocks: for example a file of historic players, someone's league and your own fantasy league.

  • Ids never clash: the added world is renumbered automatically. The Combine review page lists everything added, with ids shown as old -> new.
  • Nothing different is merged or dropped for you. Entries that are identical and clearly the same (same club in the same league of the same country, same player at the same club, and so on) merge for convenience, and are listed under Merged automatically with the reason. If one is really a different thing, select it and choose These are different: keep both.
  • The same club, player, competition or country with different data becomes a conflict. For each one, keep your version, keep the other one, keep both, or pick field by field. With many conflicts, you can choose one world as the source of truth for all those remaining; that is always your click.
  • Weaker matches are possible duplicates: a club with the same name in another division, a player with the same name, nationality and age at another club, several equally good candidates, or similar names such as "Real Madrid" and "Real Madrid CF". Both are kept; merge them or keep them separate. Each shows why it was flagged ("Matched on ...").
  • While conflicts or possible duplicates are undecided, the world cannot be saved or used to start a career. Decide each one, or choose Keep everything as it is.

Players of a club that merged into another follow the club that remains.

Save and share

Save keeps the world in your worlds, ready for a new career. Export… writes a .fmdb file anywhere, for example to share it on GitHub or Discord. From a running career, Settings → World databases → Export world… writes the world as it is now, with every club and player.

World files are plain text (JSON) written in a stable order, one entry per line, so they work well in a Git repository and can be reviewed or tweaked in a text editor. The format reference describes every field. Do not publish worlds containing real people's or clubs' data where you are not allowed to.
